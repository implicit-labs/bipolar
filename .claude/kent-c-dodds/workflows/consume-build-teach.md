# Consume, Build, Teach

Based on Kent C. Dodds' learning philosophy — the three-phase cycle that turns passive consumption into deep expertise. The philosophy: you don't truly understand something until you can teach it.

---

## Steps

### 1. Consume

Read blog posts, watch talks, take courses, read documentation. But don't stop here — most developers get stuck in an infinite consumption loop. Set a limit: one article or one video, then move to step 2.

### 2. Build

Apply what you learned in a real project. Not a tutorial clone — a project that solves a real problem for you. Building exposes the gaps in your understanding that consumption hides. When you get stuck, go back and consume more, then return to building.

### 3. Teach

Write a blog post, give a talk, record a video, or explain it to a colleague. Teaching forces you to organize your understanding and fill remaining gaps. You don't need to be an expert — teaching as a beginner helps others at the same stage. "The best time to teach is right after you've learned."

### 4. Repeat at Higher Depth

Each cycle deepens understanding. First cycle: surface-level familiarity. Second cycle: working knowledge. Third cycle: expertise. The teaching phase of each cycle becomes the consumption phase for the next person.

---

## Philosophy

> "If you can do it, you can teach it."

Teaching is not just a learning technique — it's a professional multiplier. Kent left a senior engineering role to teach full-time because teaching thousands of developers has more impact than writing code at one company. Every blog post, workshop, and course is a scaled version of mentoring.
