# Product Strategy Assessment

Based on Julian Lehr's essays — particularly "Signaling as a Service," "The Power of Defaults," and "Proof of X."

---

## Steps

### 1. Map the Layer Stack
Identify the vertical stack your product exists in. Draw it out:

```
Hardware / OS layer
Identity layer (accounts, profiles)
Application layer (your product?)
Data / Integration layer
Presentation / Interface layer
```

Where does your product sit? Who controls the layers above and below you?

### 2. Assess Default Potential
For your layer, answer:
- Who is the current default? ("If it's not in X, it doesn't exist")
- Is there scarce interface real estate? (Home screen slots, notification space, default app settings)
- Can you cultivate a belief system? ("We use Linear" as organizational identity)

Three categories of defaults:
- **Hardware/OS defaults** — hardest to displace (Apple Maps, Safari)
- **Identity layer defaults** — login providers, profile systems
- **Intersubjective defaults** — shared organizational beliefs (the most powerful kind)

### 3. Decompose the Signal
For your product's core value proposition:
- **Message:** What hidden subtext does using this product broadcast?
- **Distribution:** How does the signal reach others? (visible in meetings? shared links? physical artifacts?)
- **Amplification:** How does the user stand out from others using the same product?

### 4. Identify the Proof Mechanism
Every successful platform has one. What does using your product prove about the user?

| Platform | Proof Mechanism |
|----------|----------------|
| Twitter/X | Proof-of-writing |
| Instagram | Proof-of-photography |
| Strava | Proof-of-fitness |
| GitHub | Proof-of-code |
| **Your product** | Proof-of-??? |

The strongest platforms align status-seeking with objectively verifiable outcomes. Design for positive externalities: the signaling behavior itself should produce genuine value.

### 5. Check for Physical Touchpoints
Digital products are invisible. Can you add:
- Physical artifacts? (cards, stickers, hardware)
- Visible-in-meeting signals? (branded cursors, watermarks, "Sent via X")
- Real-world distribution? (events, merch, packaging)

### 6. Evaluate Substitution vs. Complement
Is your product trying to replace an existing behavior or add a new capability?
- **Substitutive:** High friction, users must unlearn. Usually fails.
- **Complementary:** Low friction, additive value. Usually succeeds.

If substitutive, reconsider. Can you reframe as complementary?

---

## Philosophy

> "People spend absurd amounts of money on jewellery, handbags and cars, but I can't think of a piece of software with an even remotely similar price tag."

The central unsolved problem: software lacks physical signal distribution. Every product strategy should wrestle with this gap.
