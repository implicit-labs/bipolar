# Writing Process

Based on Julian Lehr's described workflow from podcast appearances and his essays at julian.digital.

---

## Steps

### 1. Walk and Talk
Use AI voice (ChatGPT Advanced Voice, or similar) as a thinking partner while walking. Don't write yet — brainstorm, argue, explore. Voice is excellent for ideation because it's additive: you're doing something (walking) that you couldn't otherwise use for writing.

### 2. Hand-Write 60%
Write approximately 60% of the draft by hand (or keyboard). This is the core intellectual work — the frameworks, the arguments, the through-line. AI can't do this part because the value is in the thinking, not the words.

### 3. Assemble in Different Tools
Deliberately switch tools during the process. Julian uses Figma, Google Docs, and his blog CMS at different stages. The tool switch forces a perspective shift — "changing tools helps unlock creative thinking, the way some writers need a change of scenery."

### 4. Weave in References
Draw from outside tech:
- Anthropology (Girard, Harari, Dunbar)
- Economics (Hanson, Veblen, Simler)
- Pop culture (movies, games, consumer brands)

The best product insight comes from outside product.

### 5. Name Your Frameworks
Every essay should introduce or use a named model. Not "here's how signaling works" but "the Message / Distribution / Amplification framework." Named frameworks are memorable, shareable, and reusable. They become intellectual property.

### 6. Publish Infrequently, at High Signal
One deeply researched essay per quarter beats weekly blog posts. Julian's pieces circulate for years because they're dense with original thinking. Depth over frequency.

---

## Philosophy

> "In the age of AI, stories are what makes your product defensible."

The same applies to essays. AI can generate a thousand blog posts. It can't generate an original framework synthesized from anthropology and applied to product strategy. That's the moat.
