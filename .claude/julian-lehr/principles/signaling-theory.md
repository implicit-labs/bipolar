# Signaling Theory

Based on Julian Lehr's essays "Signaling as a Service" (2020), "Proof of X" (2020), "Banking on Status" (2020), and "What Shopify and Amazon Can Learn from Mimetic Theory" (2020). The philosophy: every product decision is a signaling decision, whether you designed it that way or not.

---

## Core Stance

> "We don't actually want the object itself. What we really want is to be like the person we admire."

Human desire is mimetic (Girard). Purchase decisions are driven by unconscious imitation of people we admire, not objective product attributes. This means product strategy is really identity strategy.

---

## The Signaling Framework

Every signal has three components. Optimize each independently:

### Message
The hidden subtext that using a product broadcasts. Not what the product does, but what using it says about you.

| Product | Functional Value | Signal Message |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Rolex | Tells time | "I am successful" |
| Superhuman | Sends email | "I am productive and in-the-know" |
| Patagonia | Keeps you warm | "I care about the environment" |
| Linear | Manages tasks | "My team takes craft seriously" |

### Distribution
How the signal reaches others. Physical goods have inherent distribution (people see your watch, your bag, your car). Digital products are invisible — this is software's fundamental signal problem.

Distribution mechanisms:
- **Physical artifacts** — Metal cards (N26, Apple Card), stickers, hardware
- **In-context visibility** — "Sent via Superhuman," branded share links
- **Social proof loops** — Strava leaderboards, GitHub contribution graphs
- **Word of mouth** — The weakest but most common for software

### Amplification
How you stand out from others using the same product. The difference between "I use Strava" and "I'm #1 on this segment."

Monetization insight: charge for amplification, not access. Fortnite's entire revenue is cosmetic amplification over the free base game.

## Proof-of-X Taxonomy

Every successful social platform has a proof mechanism:

| Platform | Proof Type | What It Proves |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Twitter/X | Proof-of-writing | Intellectual depth, hot takes |
| Instagram | Proof-of-photography | Aesthetic taste, lifestyle |
| TikTok | Proof-of-video | Creativity, performance |
| Strava | Proof-of-fitness | Physical discipline |
| GitHub | Proof-of-code | Technical ability |
| Goodreads | Proof-of-reading | Intellectual curiosity |

**Design principle:** The strongest platforms create positive externalities from signaling. Strava users signal fitness, but the signaling mechanism (tracking runs) also makes them healthier. This is the gold standard.

**Unsolved proofs** (product opportunities):
- Proof-of-knowledge ("Strava for Learning")
- Proof-of-prediction ("Strava for Being Right")
- Proof-of-craft (design portfolio as social platform)

## Mimetic Desire in Product

Drawing on Rene Girard via Peter Thiel:

1. We desire what others desire (mimesis)
2. We don't want the object — we want to be like the person who has it
3. This creates triangular desire: Subject → Model → Object

**For e-commerce:** Algorithmic recommendations fail because they strip out the model (the person we admire). Human curation works because it preserves the mimetic triangle. When an influencer recommends a product, the desire is: "I want to be like them" → "they use this" → "I want this."

**For product design:** Build for the model, not the object. Who are the aspirational users of your product? Make them visible. Let their usage patterns influence others.

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

| Anti-Pattern | Better Alternative |
|---|---|
| Monetizing access (paywalls) | Monetize amplification (cosmetics, premium signals) |
| Hiding who uses your product | Make power users visible — they're your signal distribution |
| Purely digital distribution | Add physical touchpoints for signal visibility |
| Algorithm-only discovery | Human curation preserves the mimetic triangle |
| Single identity model | Prismatic identity — let users be different selves in different contexts |
| Treating marketing as separate from product | Marketing is packaging design — it's part of the product |
