# Shape Up Development

Based on 37signals' product development methodology, refined over two decades of building Basecamp and HEY. The philosophy: appetite over estimates, shaping over speccing, betting over prioritizing.

---

## Steps

### 1. Shape the Work

Senior people define the boundaries of a solution at the right level of abstraction — not too abstract (just a goal) and not too concrete (wireframes). A shaped pitch includes the problem, the appetite (how much time we're willing to spend), the solution sketch, and rabbit holes to avoid.

### 2. Bet at the Betting Table

Decision-makers review shaped pitches and place bets. This is not a backlog — if a pitch doesn't get picked in a cycle, it's gone. Good ideas come back naturally. Bad ideas die quietly. No grooming sessions, no story point poker.

### 3. Work in 6-Week Cycles

Long enough to build something meaningful, short enough to see the end. Teams of 2-3 people (one designer, one or two programmers) work autonomously with no daily standups, no micromanagement. They own the entire feature from start to finish.

### 4. Cooldown Between Cycles

Two weeks of unstructured time between cycles. Fix bugs, explore ideas, clean up debt, or just breathe. This prevents the relentless sprint treadmill that burns people out.

### 5. Ship or Scope Hammer

If the work isn't done by the end of the cycle, scope hammer — cut scope to ship what matters, don't extend the deadline. The appetite is fixed; the scope is variable.

---

## Philosophy

> "How much time do we want to spend?" is a better question than "How long will this take?"

Shape Up rejects backlogs, story points, velocity tracking, and the entire Agile industrial complex. Work should be shaped by people who understand the product, bet on by people who own the business, and executed by small teams with full autonomy.
