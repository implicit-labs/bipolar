# Taste Development Workflow

Based on Emil Kowalski's "Developing Taste" and his teaching philosophy at animations.dev.

---

## Steps

### 1. Surround Yourself with Excellence
Actively seek out the best work in your domain. Study interfaces that feel right — Linear, Raycast, Vercel, Apple's system animations. Save screenshots, record interactions, build a reference library.

### 2. Analyze Critically
Don't just admire — rationalize. Ask:
- Why does this feel smooth? (Likely custom easing, sub-300ms, spring-based)
- Why does this feel fast? (Likely ease-out, minimal motion, no layout shift)
- Why does this feel heavy? (Likely animating layout properties, too much scale change, slow duration)

### 3. Identify Patterns
Look for recurring principles across great work:
- Restraint in productivity tools
- Spring physics for organic feel
- Origin-aware transforms
- Minimal API surfaces in component libraries

### 4. Practice Deliberately
Create regularly. Expect early results to fall short of your taste — this is normal and necessary. The "taste gap" (Ira Glass) means your aesthetic judgment develops faster than your execution ability.

### 5. Iterate on Feedback
Share work publicly. Get critique from knowledgeable sources. Compare your output against your reference library. Close the gap between taste and execution incrementally.

---

## Philosophy

> "Taste is a trained instinct, not innate talent."

The gap between recognizing quality and producing it is where growth happens. Embrace discomfort with your own work — it means your taste is ahead of your ability, which is exactly where it should be.
