# Ship Fast, Iterate

Based on Pieter Levels' "12 Startups in 12 Months" challenge and his MVP development methodology. The philosophy: launch at 70% completion, let paying users guide iteration.

---

## Steps

### 1. Find a Problem You Have

Look at your own daily annoyances. What do you wish existed? Travel and new environments amplify problem-spotting. The best ideas come from personal pain, not market research.

### 2. Build the MVP in Days

Sketch on paper. Build frontend in HTML/CSS first. Test responsiveness (40% of traffic is mobile). Add backend only after the frontend looks real. Use JSON text files instead of a database initially if that's faster.

### 3. Add Stripe Before Polish

Payment integration comes before authentication, before beautiful UI, before feature completeness. If people can't pay you, you can't validate the idea. Revenue is the only real signal.

### 4. Launch Everywhere

Product Hunt, Hacker News, Twitter, Reddit, niche forums. Time submissions strategically. Relaunch with major updates every 2-3 months — each relaunch is a new marketing opportunity.

### 5. Iterate on Paying Users' Feedback

Only build features that paying customers request. Ignore free users' feature requests. If nobody's paying, move on to the next idea. The 95% failure rate is normal — what matters is the 5% that works.

---

## Philosophy

> "The first version of anything always sucks. It should."

Perfectionism kills more startups than bad code. Ship fast, charge early, iterate on real feedback. If it doesn't work after a month of effort, move on. There are always more ideas.
