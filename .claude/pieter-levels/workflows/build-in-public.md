# Build in Public

Based on Pieter Levels' radical transparency approach — sharing revenue numbers, development progress, and failures in real-time. The philosophy: transparency is both accountability and marketing.

---

## Steps

### 1. Share Revenue Numbers

Put MRR in your Twitter bio. Post Stripe dashboard screenshots. Share monthly revenue updates. People are fascinated by real numbers, and transparency builds trust and following simultaneously.

### 2. Document Development in Real-Time

Tweet about features you're building. Share code snippets (not proprietary logic, but approaches). Show before/after screenshots. Make your audience feel invested in the product's evolution.

### 3. Be Honest About Failures

Share when things break. Admit when an idea doesn't work. Your 70+ failed projects aren't embarrassing — they're proof you're shipping fast enough. Failure transparency makes success stories more credible.

### 4. Use Public Accountability as a Forcing Function

The "12 Startups in 12 Months" challenge worked because it was public. Committing publicly to deadlines creates social pressure to ship. Your audience becomes your accountability partner.

### 5. Let Building in Public Be Your Marketing

Every tweet about development progress is marketing. Every revenue milestone is social proof. Every failure story is engagement. You never need paid ads if your building process itself is interesting content.

---

## Philosophy

> "I don't raise money. I do everything myself. I'm a designer, I'm the developer, I make everything."

Building in public replaces traditional marketing with authenticity. People follow the journey, not just the product. The process becomes the content, the content becomes the marketing, the marketing becomes the revenue.
